## Done
- `plan_surface(vertices, faces, coat_mask, proc, name, table_axes, *, coats=1, max_tilt_deg=60.0, blend_mm=10.0) -> Toolpath` in `src/coldspray/planner/surface.py`. It works on the selected submesh (trimesh merges the duplicate seam vertices). Parallel `mesh_plane` cuts are made `step_over_mm` apart, and `chain` joins them into polylines. `resample` puts points at most 2 mm apart. `blend` applies a Gaussian normal blend along the arc length. The gun sits at `standoff_mm` along the normal and sprays along `-normal` (built with `spray_frame`). Strokes zig-zag, open strokes are extended tangentially by `overrun_mm`, and coats alternate direction. Moves are tagged `approach`, `infill` and `turn`, and `table_axes` goes on the first PTP.
- Raster direction (`raster_axes`): strokes run along the longest side of the minimum-area rectangle of the selection, projected along the area-weighted mean normal. If |mean normal| < 0.3 (e.g. a full cylinder), the cuts are made across the longest 3D extent instead, which gives closed rings that are sprayed as loops.
- Pieces of one cut are bridged if their normals agree within 30°, for example across the test_block hole, just like a plate raster.
- Tilt limit: points more than `max_tilt_deg` from the mean normal are dropped and the stroke is split there. The count goes in `path.skipped_points` (an ad-hoc attribute on Toolpath). The limit is skipped when the mean normal is weak.

## Decisions & deviations from the plan
- The plan says "smoothed vertex normal". I used face normals blended along each stroke by a Gaussian (`blend_mm`, default 10 mm, sigma = 5 mm) instead. Vertex normals on coarse CAD tessellation (box faces with only 2 triangles) would blend across a whole face.
- For the raster direction I used a minimum-area rectangle, not PCA. PCA on the tessellation was skewed by the triangle layout and gave a tilted raster on rectangles.
- Closed cuts get no overrun.
- The skipped count is `path.skipped_points`, not a return tuple, so the signature stays `-> Toolpath`.
